# Phase 3 report: rice-yield ML dataset and evaluation methodology

Date: 2026-11-12. Companion to `phase-1-audit.md` and `phase-2-report.md`.
For: researchers and adviser. Purpose: decisions needed before any model evaluation is reported.

Nothing was changed to produce this report: no model was retrained, no record imported or edited, no prediction altered. All checks were read-only or run on copies outside the system.

## Summary

1. iClimate holds **11 genuine-looking rice observations**, not 124. They are municipal season totals for Lian, Wet Season 2020 to Wet Season 2025, from one file whose original MAO document is not in the project.
2. The 124 rows in the database are copies and demo data built from those 11 totals. Evaluating a model on them gives R² of 0.98 to 1.00. Tested honestly (one whole season held out at a time), the same models score R² of −0.05 to −0.95, which is worse than guessing the average.
3. **There is not enough verified data for a meaningful model evaluation.** The page should say so instead of showing scores.
4. The Model Evaluation page shows no scores today, but one of its sections already shows a misleading figure taken from demo data (section 8).
5. Six decisions are needed from you (section 10).

**One open question about the monthly file.** The remark "Typhoon Paeng losses" appears on all five planting months of Wet Season 2022, and "Typhoon Kristine losses" on all five of Wet Season 2024, with low yields in every month including May. Both typhoons are understood to have struck in late October. Rice planted in May would normally be harvested before then. Either the losses were recorded for the whole season and spread across months, or the "month" column is not the planting month. MAO needs to confirm which.

## 3. Planting and harvest periods

The file records planting months only. **No harvest date is recorded anywhere for the MAO data.**

| Season label | Stored in database as | Planting months in the file |
|---|---|---|
| WS 2020 … WS 2025 | Wet, year of planting | May to September of that year |
| DS 2020-21 … DS 2024-25 | Dry, **first** year (DS 2020-21 is stored as "Dry 2020") | October of the first year to March of the next |

No planting is recorded for April in any year.

Harvest periods can only be estimated by assuming a crop duration. The demo seeder assumes 110 to 123 days; that is its own assumption, not MAO data. Under it, wet-season harvests would fall roughly from late August to January, and dry-season harvests from late January to July. These ranges are shown only to make the question concrete and are not used anywhere.

Two calendars in iClimate disagree with the file:

- The iClimate season calendar counts October as wet. The MAO file counts October as the first dry-season planting month. (The deployed yield model was trained with wet = May to September, which matches the MAO file.)
- The dataset builder matches rice to climate by calendar year and season name. For "Dry 2020" it would use February to April 2020 and November to December 2020. The actual crop was planted October 2020 to March 2021.

## 4. Climate matching windows compared

Each option below is a possible answer to "which months of weather belong to this rice figure?". Rainfall totals are from the Ambulong station, shown for three seasons to illustrate how much the choice matters.

| Option | Wet season months | Dry season months | DS 2020-21 rain | WS 2022 rain | WS 2024 rain | Seasons with a missing month |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Planting months only | May–Sep | Oct–Mar | 1,185 mm | 1,338 mm | 1,244 mm | 0 |
| B. Planting through last likely harvest (needs a crop duration) | May–Jan | Oct–Jul | 2,060 mm | 2,212 mm | 2,167 mm | 1 (WS 2025 needs January 2026) |
| C. iClimate season calendar | May–Oct | Nov–Apr | 715 mm | 1,813 mm | 1,914 mm | 0 |
| D. Per planting month: that month plus the growing months after it | varies | varies | varies | varies | varies | depends on duration |

What can be said without assuming any agronomy:

- The same season's rainfall differs by up to about three times between options. The choice changes the dataset more than the choice of algorithm does.
- Option A leaves out October for wet-season crops, the month in which both recorded typhoon losses occurred.
- Option B windows of neighbouring seasons overlap by several months, so the seasons stop being separate observations.
- Option C does not follow the MAO season definition.
- Option D is the only one that follows each planting month separately, but it requires a crop duration agreed with MAO.
- In every option the weather is from Ambulong station in Tanauan, not from Lian. One weather value applies to the whole municipality.

No option is recommended here. This is a decision for the researchers and MAO.

## 5. The independent unit for evaluation

| Candidate unit | Count | Independent? |
|---|---|---|
| Barangay-season rows in the database | 110 | **No.** Ten copies of each season with identical yield and identical weather. |
| Municipal planting month | 60 | **Partly.** Months in one season share the same weather period and the same typhoon. Only usable if MAO confirms the monthly figures were recorded separately (see the open question in section 2). |
| Municipal season | 11 | **Yes.** This is the unit that can be defended now. |

Recommendation: treat the **municipal season** as the independent unit (11 observations). If MAO confirms the monthly figures, the 60 planting months may be used as rows, but they must always be grouped by season when testing.

## 6. Leakage-free validation

Recommended rules, in order of importance:

1. **Never split at random by row.** All rows of one season go together, either all in training or all in testing.
2. **Leave one season out** (11 rounds), or **forward in time**: train on all earlier seasons and test on the next one, with a minimum number of training seasons agreed in advance.
3. **Compare against a simple baseline**: the average yield of the training seasons. A model that cannot beat this has no demonstrated skill.
4. **Report MAE in t/ha first.** With 11 observations R² is unstable and should not be the headline figure.
5. **Weather used for a season must not include months after that season's harvest.**
6. **State the number of independent seasons beside every score.**

The current evaluation uses random 5-fold splitting by row and breaks rules 1, 3 and 6.

## 7. Is there enough verified data?

No.

- 11 independent observations over 6 years. The evaluation code's own reliability floor is 30.
- None of the rice records is validated.
- There are no Lian climate observations. The only climate history is one station outside Lian.
- Two of the 11 seasons are typhoon-loss seasons, and WS 2024 (1.90 t/ha) lies far below the rest (3.40 to 4.35). A few seasons decide the result.
- Evidence from an isolated test on copies of the data, using the current seven features and planting-month weather (Option A):

| Test method | Linear Regression R² | Random Forest R² | Gradient Boosting R² |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current method (random split of 110 rows) | 0.87 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| One whole season held out | −0.05 | −0.95 | −0.83 |

The high scores come entirely from the same season appearing in both training and testing. This agrees with the Phase 2 finding that the candidate model's test R² is −1.10.

## 8. Audit of the Model Evaluation page

**Scores shown today:** none. The climate table is empty, so the page shows "Evaluation needs more data" and 0 matched samples. All 1,360 completed evaluation runs since 2026-09-29 produced 0 rows.

**What would go wrong as soon as any climate record is matched:**

- Nothing checks for duplicated or derived rows. The page would show 110 or 124 "Matched Samples".
- The evaluation code produces a warning below 30 rows, but the page never displays it.
- R² is printed to six decimal places, a "Best Performing Algorithm" is highlighted, and the text beside it reads "Defense-ready summary for the manuscript objective".
- The 14 demo rice rows are not excluded.

**Misleading now:** the section "Data for Future Retraining" states **5,794 corrections on record, average miss 0.14 t/ha**. All 5,794 are demo records in which the predicted yield was generated as the actual yield plus 0.12. The page describes them as "harvests where a farmer recorded the actual yield". The same summary is also read by the MAO home page, the yield prediction page, the model feedback service and the manuscript report builder.

## 9. Showing "Insufficient verified data"

Recommended behaviour for the Model Evaluation page:

- Show metrics only when all of these hold: the rice records used are validated by MAO; demo and derived rows are excluded; the count of independent seasons meets an agreed minimum; the test method is season-grouped or forward in time.
- Otherwise show one clear statement in place of the metrics table, the best-algorithm tile and the comparison tool:

  > **Insufficient verified data.** Model accuracy cannot be evaluated yet.
  > Independent seasons available: 11 (unvalidated). Minimum needed: *[agreed number]*.
  > Lian climate observations: none. Climate history available: Ambulong station (Tanauan) only.

- Show "Not available" for best algorithm, never a name.
- Remove "Defense-ready summary" wording while no valid evaluation exists.
- In "Data for Future Retraining", count only harvests from real farmer accounts. With none, show "No farmer has recorded a harvest with a predicted yield yet".
- When metrics are eventually shown: state the number of independent seasons, the test method, the baseline result, and the climate source by name.
